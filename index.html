The simple way to learn how to draw

Drawing is a skill, not a talent — and you can learn it the same way you'd learn to type or ride a bike: a little theory, and lots of short, focused practice. Draw.tips gives you exactly that. Five short lessons, a practice timer, and printable worksheets. No sign-up, no fluff, free.

Why Draw.tips

Most beginners don't quit drawing.
They quit choosing tutorials.

There are thousands of drawing tutorials out there, and that's the problem: "where do I even start?" is the most common question new artists ask. Draw.tips removes the choice. One path, in order, with the practice built in.

1

Read a short lesson

Each lesson is a 5–10 minute read that teaches one core idea — how to see, how to move your arm, how to build forms. No hour-long videos.

2

Do the exercise

Every lesson ends with one concrete exercise. Print the matching worksheet or use blank paper — 10 to 15 minutes and you're done for the day.

3

Practice daily

The Practice Room gives you a warm-up timer and a drawing prompt of the day, so you always know what to draw — the habit is the skill.

Daily Practice

Never wonder what to draw again

Lessons teach you how to draw; practice makes it stick. The Practice Room has two free tools that working artists use every day, built right into the site.

⏱ Warm-up Timer

A guided warm-up session: the timer calls out line, ellipse and box exercises in timed rounds, just like a gesture-drawing class. Pick 5, 10 or 15 minutes and go.

🎲 Prompt of the Day

One drawing prompt every day, the same for everyone — plus a shuffle button when you want more. Small, concrete subjects matched to the skills from the lessons.

🖨 Printable Worksheets

Guided practice sheets for lines, ellipses and boxes that you can print at home. Trace, repeat, and watch your lines straighten out within a week.
